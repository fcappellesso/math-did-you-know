# 📚 Reliable theses and dissertations

Where to find theses and dissertations **from official sources**, **free and
legally**, and how to check whether a text is used by the community. Every
address below belongs to a university, a national library, a government or a
scientific institution.

---

## 🌍 International

1. **OATD:** search millions of open-access theses and dissertations from all
   over the world, at [oatd.org](https://oatd.org).
2. **NDLTD:** the international network of digital libraries of theses, at
   [ndltd.org](https://ndltd.org).
3. **BASE:** Bielefeld University's academic search engine; use the filter
   "Document type → thesis", at [base-search.net](https://www.base-search.net).
4. **CORE:** gathers open-access texts from repositories worldwide, at
   [core.ac.uk](https://core.ac.uk).
5. **ProQuest Dissertations & Theses Global:** the largest thesis database,
   mainly from the United States. It is paid; access is through your
   university library when it subscribes
   ([learn more](https://about.proquest.com/en/products-services/pqdtglobal/)).
6. **arXiv:** many authors post their thesis there, at
   [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org).
7. **Mathematics Genealogy Project:** lists the thesis title, university and
   advisor of each doctor in mathematics, at
   [mathgenealogy.org](https://www.mathgenealogy.org).
8. **Google Scholar:** finds theses, but always check that the link leads to
   the official repository, at [scholar.google.com](https://scholar.google.com).

## 🇪🇺 Europe

9. **Europe in general:** DART-Europe, the European thesis portal, at
   [dart-europe.org](https://www.dart-europe.org).
10. **Germany:** every German thesis is deposited at the Deutsche
    Nationalbibliothek, at [dnb.de](https://www.dnb.de).
11. **Kassel:** the official repository of the University of Kassel is KOBRA,
    at [kobra.uni-kassel.de](https://kobra.uni-kassel.de).
12. **France:** the national catalogue of doctoral theses, at
    [theses.fr](https://theses.fr).
13. **France (full text):** the thesis portal of the HAL open archive, at
    [theses.hal.science](https://theses.hal.science).
14. **Spain:** the official TESEO database of the Ministry of Education, at
    [educacion.gob.es/teseo](https://www.educacion.gob.es/teseo).
15. **Catalonia:** the TDX open-access thesis repository, at
    [tdx.cat](https://www.tdx.cat).
16. **Portugal:** RCAAP gathers the country's open-access scientific
    repositories, at [rcaap.pt](https://www.rcaap.pt).
17. **Italy:** doctoral theses are deposited by law at the National Central
    Libraries of Florence and Rome, at
    [bncf.firenze.sbn.it](https://www.bncf.firenze.sbn.it); many universities
    also have their own repositories.
18. **Switzerland:** the ETH Zurich Research Collection, at
    [research-collection.ethz.ch](https://www.research-collection.ethz.ch).
19. **Sweden and the Nordic countries:** the DiVA portal, at
    [diva-portal.org](https://www.diva-portal.org).
20. **United Kingdom (Cambridge):** the Apollo repository, at
    [repository.cam.ac.uk](https://www.repository.cam.ac.uk).
21. **United Kingdom (Oxford):** the ORA repository, at
    [ora.ox.ac.uk](https://ora.ox.ac.uk).

## 🌎 The Americas

22. **United States (MIT):** the DSpace@MIT repository, at
    [dspace.mit.edu](https://dspace.mit.edu).
23. **Canada:** the Theses Canada service of the national library (Library and
    Archives Canada), at
    [library-archives.canada.ca](https://library-archives.canada.ca).
24. **Latin America:** the LA Referencia network, which gathers repositories
    from several countries, at [lareferencia.info](https://www.lareferencia.info).
25. **Mexico:** UNAM's thesis catalogue, TESIUNAM, at
    [tesiunam.dgb.unam.mx](https://tesiunam.dgb.unam.mx).

## 🌏 Asia and Oceania

26. **India:** the national repository Shodhganga, at
    [shodhganga.inflibnet.ac.in](https://shodhganga.inflibnet.ac.in).
27. **Japan:** CiNii Research, which includes Japanese doctoral theses, at
    [cir.nii.ac.jp](https://cir.nii.ac.jp).
28. **Australia:** Trove, from the National Library of Australia, at
    [trove.nla.gov.au](https://trove.nla.gov.au).

## 🇧🇷 Brazil

29. **Brazil in general:** the Brazilian Digital Library of Theses and
    Dissertations (BDTD), from IBICT, at [bdtd.ibict.br](https://bdtd.ibict.br).
30. **CAPES catalogue:** every thesis and dissertation defended in Brazilian
    graduate programmes, at
    [catalogodeteses.capes.gov.br](https://catalogodeteses.capes.gov.br).
31. **Oasisbr:** the Brazilian open-access portal, gathering repositories from
    the whole country, at [oasisbr.ibict.br](https://oasisbr.ibict.br).
32. **UnB:** the institutional repository of the University of Brasília
    (RIUnB), at [repositorio.unb.br](https://repositorio.unb.br).
33. **USP:** the Digital Library of Theses and Dissertations of the University
    of São Paulo, at [teses.usp.br](https://www.teses.usp.br).
34. **IMPA:** dissertations and theses defended at the institute are listed in
    the graduate section of its website, at [impa.br](https://impa.br).
35. **SciELO:** for the papers that often grow out of theses, at
    [scielo.org](https://www.scielo.org).
36. **Lattes:** shows the publications, supervisions and examination
    committees of every Brazilian researcher, at
    [lattes.cnpq.br](https://lattes.cnpq.br).

## 🔓 How to get access for free, within the law

37. **CAPES Journal Portal (Brazil):** sign in with your university's
    institutional login (CAFe) and read paid journals and databases for free,
    at [periodicos.capes.gov.br](https://www.periodicos.capes.gov.br).
38. **Unpaywall:** a browser extension that shows, when you open a paper,
    whether a legal free version exists, at [unpaywall.org](https://unpaywall.org).
39. **Open Access Button:** looks for legal free versions of papers and, if
    there are none, helps you request them from the author, at
    [openaccessbutton.org](https://openaccessbutton.org).
40. **DOAJ:** the official directory of open-access scientific journals, at
    [doaj.org](https://doaj.org).
41. **COMUT (IBICT, Brazil):** the Brazilian document-delivery service, which
    obtains copies of documents from other libraries within the law, at
    [comut.ibict.br](https://comut.ibict.br).
42. **Ask the author:** writing to the author for a copy is legal, common and
    almost always welcome. The e-mail address is usually in the paper itself
    or on the researcher's web page.
43. **Your university library:** interlibrary loan can obtain books and theses
    that your institution does not hold.

## ✅ How to check whether a text is reliable

44. **Citations:** see how many times the text has been cited in
    [MathSciNet](https://mathscinet.ams.org) (through institutional access) or
    in [zbMATH Open](https://zbmath.org) (free).
45. **Did it become a paper?** Check whether the results of the thesis were
    later published in a peer-reviewed journal.
46. **Advisor and committee:** check who supervised and who examined the work,
    in the Mathematics Genealogy Project or, for Brazil, in Lattes.
47. **Permanent link:** prefer citing the official repository address, with a
    DOI or *Handle*, which does not change over time.
48. **Licence:** before sharing the PDF, check the licence stated in the
    repository (for example, Creative Commons).

## ⚠️ An important caution

49. **Avoid unofficial websites:** sites that distribute texts without
    authorization infringe copyright and may carry viruses. The sources in
    this list are enough to find almost everything freely and legally.

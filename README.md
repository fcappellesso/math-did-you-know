## 1. How logins work

Not every website requires an account. There are four kinds of access,
marked in the tables with these symbols:

| Symbol | Kind of access | What to do |
| :---: | --- | --- |
| 🆓 | **Free, no login** | Just open the website. |
| 👤 | **Free personal account** | Sign up, preferably with your **institutional e-mail**: many websites use the university domain to confirm a research affiliation. |
| 🏛️ | **Institutional access** | The website is paid, but universities subscribe to it. Access works through the university network, VPN or institutional login (in Brazil, through the CAPES Journal Portal; see [section 14](#14-for-researchers-in-brazil)). |
| 💳 | **Paid** | Membership fee or subscription, almost always with a **student discount**. |

> **Tip:** use a password manager (the browser's own is fine) and keep all
> your identifiers in one place: ORCID, Google Scholar, Scopus, MathSciNet…

## 2. Where to start: a checklist

A suggested order, from most important to optional:

- [ ] **ORCID**: create your permanent researcher identifier.
- [ ] **Institutional access**: test access to paid databases from off campus.
- [ ] **Google Scholar**: create a profile and set up alerts.
- [ ] **arXiv**: create an account and subscribe to the daily listing of your categories.
- [ ] **zbMATH Open** and **MathSciNet**: check whether an author profile already exists.
- [ ] **MathOverflow** and **Math.SE**: create a Stack Exchange account.
- [ ] **Journal alerts**: choose three or four journals in your research area.
- [ ] **ResearchGate**: create a profile (optional).
- [ ] **Societies**: consider membership (discounts on meetings and books).

## 3. Academic identity

Profiles that identify a researcher and gather their work.

| Website | What it is | Access | How to take part |
| --- | --- | :---: | --- |
| [ORCID](https://orcid.org) | A unique, permanent researcher identifier (a number like `0000-0002-…`). It avoids confusion between similar names and is requested by journals at submission and by funding agencies. | 👤 | Sign up under *Register*, add education and affiliation, and authorize the connection with Scopus, Web of Science and other systems so that publications are added automatically. |
| [Google Scholar](https://scholar.google.com) | Article search and researcher profiles with citation counts. | 👤 | Under *My profile*, create a profile with a Google account, confirm your institutional e-mail and add your publications. **Alerts** (the envelope icon) e-mail you new papers on a topic or by an author. |
| [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net) | Academic social network: profile, publications, followers and a Q&A section. | 👤 | Sign up with an institutional e-mail. Follow researchers and share versions of your papers when the journal allows it. |
| [Academia.edu](https://www.academia.edu) | Similar to ResearchGate, more popular in the humanities. | 👤 | Optional. |
| [Web of Science Researcher Profiles](https://www.webofscience.com) | Author profile in the Web of Science database (Clarivate). It replaced Publons and also records peer reviews done for journals. | 👤 + 🏛️ | The profile is free; full database search requires institutional access. |
| [Scopus Author ID](https://www.scopus.com) | Author identifier in the Scopus database (Elsevier), created **automatically** from your first indexed publication. | 🏛️ | Nothing to create: after your first paper, search for your name, check the data and link the profile to ORCID. |
| [Mathematics Genealogy Project](https://www.mathgenealogy.org) | The family tree of mathematics: who advised whom, going back centuries. | 🆓 | After the PhD defense, submit your data through the website's form. |

## 4. Databases and literature search

| Website | What it is | Access | How to use it |
| --- | --- | :---: | --- |
| [arXiv (math)](https://arxiv.org/archive/math) | The preprint server: almost every mathematics paper appears here before (or besides) the journal. Categories follow the subject areas, for example `math.AP` (analysis of PDEs), `math.DS` (dynamical systems) and `math.FA` (functional analysis). | 🆓 to read · 👤 to submit | Reading is free. To receive new papers by e-mail every day, subscribe under *Help → Subscribe*. **Submitting** requires an account, and first-time submitters to a category may need an *endorsement* from someone who already publishes there. |
| [MathSciNet](https://mathscinet.ams.org) | The American Mathematical Society database: reviews of almost every mathematics paper, with author profiles and citations. | 🏛️ / 💳 | Through your university network. Author search shows your *MR Author ID*. |
| [zbMATH Open](https://zbmath.org) | The European counterpart of MathSciNet, **free** since 2021. Supports formula search and search by MSC classification. | 🆓 | No account needed. Errors in an author profile can be corrected through a form. |
| [Web of Science](https://www.webofscience.com) | Multidisciplinary database with citation indexes and journal impact factors. | 🏛️ | Useful to find out who cited a paper. |
| [Scopus](https://www.scopus.com) | Elsevier's multidisciplinary database, with journal and author metrics. | 🏛️ | Also shows citations and author profiles. |
| [OEIS](https://oeis.org) | The On-Line Encyclopedia of Integer Sequences. | 🆓 · 👤 to contribute | Type the first terms of a sequence and find out what it is. |
| [nLab](https://ncatlab.org) | A wiki for advanced mathematics (categories, topology, mathematical physics). | 🆓 | Free to read. |
| [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov) | The main database of health research, including studies on acupuncture and herbal medicine. | 🆓 · 👤 optional | A free NCBI account lets you save searches and receive alerts. |

> ⚠️ **Predatory journals:** be wary of journals not indexed in the databases
> above and of e-mail invitations to publish "quickly" for a fee.

## 8. Journals

**How to follow a journal:** on the journal's website, look for *"Sign up for
alerts"*, *"Table of contents alerts"* or *"RSS"* to receive the contents of
each new issue by e-mail. This usually needs a free account with the
publisher (Springer, Elsevier, Wiley…). Full texts usually require
institutional access 🏛️.

## 15. Traditional Chinese Medicine

There is no Traditional Chinese Medicine community on Stack Exchange. One can
be proposed through [Area 51](https://area51.stackexchange.com), but a
proposal only becomes a site after gathering many committed users and being
approved, which is rare nowadays. For visibility, the best approach is to
**go where the experts already are**:

| Place | Why | Access |
| --- | --- | :---: |
| [知乎 Zhihu](https://www.zhihu.com) | The "Chinese Stack Exchange", with many TCM doctors and teachers answering questions. The best option for readers of Chinese. | 👤 (phone sign-up) |
| [Reddit · r/Acupuncture](https://www.reddit.com/r/Acupuncture/) | Discussions in English with practicing acupuncturists. | 👤 |
| Acupuncture associations and schools | Forums and groups (Telegram, WhatsApp) with professionals in the field. | By invitation or membership |
| [Medical Sciences SE](https://medicalsciences.stackexchange.com) | Questions with a scientific focus (evidence, studies). | 👤 |
| [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov) | To search published research on acupuncture and herbal medicine. | 🆓 |
| A personal website on GitHub Pages | A **showcase** for ideas, texts and cases, linking to the discussions in the places above. | 👤 |

> ⚠️ **Clinical records and case reports:** publish only **fully anonymized**
> cases, with no name, date, place or any detail that could identify the
> patient (data-protection law and professional ethics).
